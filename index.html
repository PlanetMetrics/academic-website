About

About

I am a PhD student at the University of California. I am also a registered landscape architect (Mississippi #728) and an ISA Certified Arborist (WE-13893A). I hold an Advanced Permaculture Certification in Teacher Training from the Permaculture Institute of North America. I am interested in a tenure-track faculty position in landscape architecture or sustainability.

Positionality Statement

My research and praxis are shaped by my experiences growing up in Kaohsiung, a port city where hardworking communities live alongside industry and pollution. My worldview is shaped by Austronesian and Holo Taiwanese perspectives. I was also given the Rukai name Daravake by elders.

My undergraduate training was in agribusiness, horticultural science, and agro-food systems. During that time, I was deeply inspired by the work of Spoonbill Action Voluntary Echo International. I later received a Master of Landscape Architecture, with a concentration in landscape planning, from the University of Oregon, where I was advised by Professor Yekang Ko.

I am now a PhD student at the University of California, where I conduct research in the Spatial Climate Solutions Lab, advised by Professor Grace Wu. At the University of California, my MS training emphasises environmental econometrics and data science.

I approach environmental change through spatial analysis, modelling, data science, design, and site engineering, alongside community advocacy and participatory planning. My current and past work focuses on coastal environmental change in southwestern Taiwan, stakeholder engagement in electricity grid planning, microgrids for disaster resilience, and agrivoltaic development and expansion.

I see stewardship of the commons as an ongoing practice of care and shared responsibility.
